# Sovereignty Assessment: design

Version 1.0.0, 20 November 2026. How the interface looks and why. The look is Vici, Victor's design system, copied unchanged into `vici/`, through the sister prototypes' shared shell (REVIEW.md). The screens of the finished prototype are sketched in PLAN.md, section f; version 0.1.0 built the header, the control bar, the catalogue in the centrepiece, the console with its rail, the drawer and the dialogs, version 0.2.0 adds the sovereignty map, the drawer's panels and the run in the console (§9), version 0.3.0 adds Compare, the Portfolio, the export card and the method view (§10), and version 1.0.0 makes it hold from 1920×1080 down to 1280×720 and by keyboard (§11).

## 1. Principles

- **Made for a room.** The prototype is shown on a projector to public-sector decision makers. It reads from the back of a room at 1920×1080 and stays usable down to 1280×720, without horizontal scrolling.
- **Honest at a glance.** "You are interacting with an AI system" is always visible; the header always carries "Prototype – not an official service of the German Federal Government"; every score says "indicative"; a figure without a basis says "no data" and is never drawn as 0; every finding will show its sources. No federal eagle, no ministry or service logo, nothing that imitates an official site.
- **The person decides.** The agent researches and proposes; a person reviews, chooses and approves. Nothing is sent.
- **Calm.** Light theme by default (lit rooms), dark through Vici's toggle. Motion only where something happens.

## 2. Layout

The sister apps' grid: header, control bar, workspace and drawer fill the window; only panels scroll.

- **Header:** the page icon and the name with the prototype line under it; the mode badge, "How it works", the theme toggle, the EN and DE switch and the AI disclosure; the four tiles (overall score, weakest critical link, evidence coverage, exclusive non-EU control).
- **Control bar:** the service field with suggestions from the catalogue ("Enter a base service or platform"), Start agent, the model, the replay speed, the run's status, Abort, Reset and Diagnostics.
- **Workspace:** the centrepiece (60 %) and the console (40 %). Before a run the centrepiece shows the catalogue as chips grouped by category, and the chosen entry's description, status and starting sources; the sovereignty map takes its place in version 0.2.0. The console holds the progress rail with the agent's five steps and the log.
- **Drawer:** Building blocks, Review, Levers, Outbox and Audit, with Vici's byline in the middle and "Drafts only. Nothing is sent." beside the toggle. Until the agent arrives, each tab says what it will hold. From version 1.0.0 its panel opens beside the map, over the console's column, and the tabs stay on their bar at the bottom (§11).

## 3. Colour grammar

Four rules of meaning, the sister apps' grammar applied to this domain:

1. **Merlot is the person in charge:** Start agent, Approve, the chosen catalogue entry, the step that waits for a person, the focus ring. It never encodes a state.
2. **Lilac is the AI's voice:** the AI disclosure, AI labels, narration, the agent's levers, and the page icon's cell.
3. **Fjord is checking:** searches and fetches pulse in fjord as an outline, never as a fill, so a pulse never reads as a level.
4. **Ink and Vici's neutrals carry everything else.** Danger, warning and success mark state only, always with a word or a mark (Diagnostics' Pass, Fail and Check tags carry their icon and their word). A neutral tape marks Simulation.

The levels use Vici's diverging scale with the level number and the Made-in and Run-by codes written in each honeycomb cell: level 0 `--vc-div-1`, 1 `--vc-div-3`, 2 `--vc-div-4`, 3 `--vc-div-5`, 4 `--vc-div-7`, with `--vc-text-inverse` on levels 0 and 4 and `--vc-text` on levels 1 to 3. `--vc-div-2` and `--vc-div-6` stay unused, because no text token reaches 4.5:1 on them in both themes (PLAN.md f.3). The assessed service is drawn in teal (`--vc-data-2` with `--vc-data-pastel-2`) and a compared one in apricot (`--vc-data-3`). `tests/tokens.mjs` keeps every pair the app draws at its contrast in both themes, with the pairs listed in `tests/lib/prototype.mjs`.

The intro window's five steps take the same grammar: lilac where the AI works on its own (Understand), fjord where it checks (Research), neutral where code computes (Score), merlot where the person decides (Your review), ink for the levers (Recommend).

## 4. Hexagons

Hexagons replace circles everywhere, with no exception: Vici's cells, chips, tags, stepper markers, status dots and avatars are hexagons, and so are the spider diagram's rings and markers and the honeycomb's cells. `tests/hexagon.mjs` scans the app's files and the rendered app in both themes, through the start screen, the catalogue and its suggestions, Diagnostics, the mode and key dialogs, every drawer tab and the German interface, and fails on anything round.

## 5. Type and sizes

Vici's fonts, served from `vici/` (the security policy allows no font from another site), on the sister apps' fluid root size: 15 px at 1280 px wide, about 17 px at 1920 px, never above 18 px, and it follows the reader's own text-size setting. The tiles' figures use 2.125rem (1.875rem below 1,700 px). Every control the room clicks is at least 40 px high: the service field, the catalogue's chips and the language switch are 44 px or 40 px.

## 6. The header and the prototype line

At 1920×1080 the header is one row: the brand with its two lines, the badges in two rows, and the four tiles, each at least 176 px wide so a German note fits its two lines. Up to 1,700 px wide the tiles take a row of their own under the brand and the badges, so neither the prototype line, the badges nor a tile's text is squeezed or cut; the header grows to three short rows, as PLAN.md f.2 sketches (until version 1.0.0 this held below 1,500 px, and between 1,501 and 1,700 px the badges wrapped to three rows and a German note was cut). The prototype line wraps rather than being cut to an ellipsis at any width. The control bar drops its labels on laptops (the sister apps' breakpoints), and Reset and Diagnostics keep their names in their tooltips.

## 7. Two languages in the interface

The EN and DE switch is two toggle buttons with `aria-pressed`, drawn as the shell's segmented control beside the theme toggle. Each names its language in that language ("English (EN)", "Deutsch (DE)"). A switch changes every word at once without a reload; what was typed, what is chosen and where a dialog stands stay. German texts address the reader formally (Sie) and use German quotation marks („…“). The catalogue's names and descriptions come from the catalogue in both languages. From version 0.2.0 the layers carry the Deutschland-Stack's German labels in German and the brief's English labels in English, with the German original as a tooltip.

## 8. Deviations from Vici and the sister apps

- **Teal for the assessed service.** Vici's data series start with merlot, but merlot means the person in charge here, so the assessed service takes the second series colour (teal) and a compared service the third (apricot). The brief asks for this record.
- **The catalogue's chips are radio buttons.** Vici's toggle chips (`aria-pressed`) let a second click undo the choice, and vici.js toggles them by itself; one service at a time is a single choice, so each chip wraps a radio button, Vici's other chip pattern, with its tick and the arrow keys across the whole catalogue.
- **A row of its own for the tiles up to 1,700 px** (§6), where the sister apps keep the tiles beside the badges down to 1,180 px: this app's header carries a two-line prototype line and a language switch the others do not have.
- **The drawer beside the map** (§11): the sister apps' drawer opens at the bottom; here its panel opens over the console's column, because this app's centrepiece is a diagram that needs its height. The shared `css/drawer.css` is unchanged; the rule is in `css/panels.css`.
- **The intro's step tones** are this app's (§3): the shared stylesheet's tones name the Timetable Translator's steps.
- **The person's turn on the rail** replaces the step's label with the merlot tag ("Your review") instead of standing beside it: this app's review step is itself called "Your review", and five steps with a tag beside one do not fit the console's width. The rule is in `css/panels.css`; the shared rail is unchanged.
- **The byline's label** gives way below 1,340 px instead of the shared stylesheet's 1,180 px, because the drawer's tabs now carry counts and the bar must stay one row in German (`css/panels.css`).

## 9. The sovereignty map and the drawer (version 0.2.0)

**The map** takes the centrepiece once a run starts; the catalogue gives way and returns with Reset.

- **The spider diagram** opens by layer: six axes in the portal's order (Infrastructure, Development, security and operations, Platform, Base services, Applications and services, Interface and access), so the web is a hexagon; "By building block" draws one axis per block at 25 × its level, labelled with its id, its two codes and its name. Rings mark the levels; a layer or block without a score is a dashed axis with its status in muted italics, never a zero. The service is teal on its pastel fill (§8). The diagram is drawn at its host's size in pixels: the web takes the room its labels leave, and the labels keep their size, so a short map (a laptop screen, the drawer open) shows a smaller web with labels that stay legible rather than shrinking everything; a very small web drops the ring labels.
- **The Made-in and Run-by mix** stands beside it: one bar per origin class, sorted by count, the class named by its code and, on hover and for screen readers, in words, with the critical blocks' count.
- **The honeycomb** below groups the blocks by layer, one hexagon cell per block, filled on the diverging scale (§3) with the level and both codes written in the cell; a gap is an empty cell with a dashed outline; "?" marks a partly unknown level. A short map folds it into one strip per layer with small cells that keep their level. The groups share their rows: every head ends on one line, so every group's cells start on one line, however many lines a head wraps to.
- **The table** is one click away and holds the same numbers: the layer scores with their statuses and the block levels with their classes.
- **Searching and reading** pulse a fjord outline around the map, never a fill; the console's pages-read count says the same in words.

**The console** carries the agent's narration with the lilac AI label, code's own lines without it, each tool call as one plain sentence with its details one click away, and the run facts underneath (calls against the cap, the cost, searches, pages read). In Simulation the neutral tape names the pre-run's date and the word SIMULATION stands faintly behind the log.

**The drawer's tabs** follow the run: Building blocks (the blocks with filters; each opens its evidence: the quote, the source, the date read and the fields it supports, and marks unverified fields), Review, Levers, Outbox and Audit. Merlot is the person in charge: the Review's Confirm, a block's "corrected by reviewer" tag, the levers' one Approve. A tab that needs the person shows its count in merlot and opens by itself; the others stay quiet. The Outbox's drafts carry "Draft. Nothing is sent." and the AI label; a draft in the other language says so.

## 10. Compare, the Portfolio, the exports and the method (version 0.3.0)

**Three views of the centrepiece.** A segmented control in the head of the visible view switches between the assessment (the map of §9), Compare and the Portfolio, each with its Vici icon (honeycomb, layers, chart) and its name; on narrow maps only the icons remain, with the names as labels for screen readers. The switch moves with the view, so it always sits where the eye already is.

**Compare** puts the spider diagram first and full width, then the table. The first assessment keeps the map's series: teal (`--vc-data-2`) with a solid outline on its pastel fill. The second is apricot (`--vc-data-3`) with a dashed outline on a lighter pastel fill and hollow markers, so the two series tell apart without colour, in print and under forced colours (where both fall back to the system's text colour and only the dash and the hollow markers remain). The legend repeats the line styles. An axis is dashed only where neither assessment has a score. The table sets the two side by side in the same order as the axes, with the overall score, the weakest link, the coverage, exclusive non-EU control and a difference column in plain signed numbers.

**The Portfolio** is three readings of the loaded set. The bar chart sorts the overall scores from high to low, one teal bar per assessment with its score written at the end, and "no data" last. Exclusive non-EU control per category is a number per category, never a colour. The Made-in mix is a waffle of hexagons, one cell per block, grouped and labelled by origin class in the classes' order: Germany in `--vc-data-4`; another EU state in its pastel with the same outline, because the method scores them alike (D2) while the display keeps them apart; the US in `--vc-data-6`; China in its pastel with the same outline; elsewhere in `--vc-data-8`; unknown as an empty cell with a dashed `--vc-border-strong` outline, like a gap in the honeycomb. The data colours are categorical here and never mean a level: levels keep the diverging scale of §3. Below the charts, the loaded set is a table with each item's origin (pre-run, this session, file) and a remove button.

**The export card** sits at the top of the Outbox: four buttons, each naming the file it will save, disabled with the reason when there is nothing to save yet. The saved HTML report uses Vici's light colours written out, so it prints as it shows.

**The method view** is a wide dialog with the six layers as a table in the portal's order, the levels with the SEAL list, the score rules, the weights, the classes, the evidence rules and what the score is not. The weights are six menus and a Reset button; a change redraws only that section and keeps the focus where it was. The overall tile in the header opens the same dialog: it is a button now, with the same look as the other tiles and a pointer.

## 11. Projector sizes, the keyboard and screen readers (version 1.0.0)

**The drawer beside the map.** With the drawer at the bottom, an open drawer left the map about 370 px at 1920×1080 and about 130 px at 1280×720, where the spider and the honeycomb did not show at all. From 1,101 px wide, wherever the workspace has two columns, the drawer's panel now opens over the console's column, as high as the workspace: the map keeps its full height beside the review, the levers or the Outbox, and the person sees the blocks they are judging. The console waits behind the panel, hidden so the keyboard does not reach it, and comes back with Hide; a new run, or a warning the console writes, folds the drawer away by itself so the console is never covered when it speaks. The workspace's columns move to 54 and 46 per cent while the panel is open. The panel follows the console's box through CSS anchor positioning (current Chrome and Edge, the browsers the README names); a browser without it opens the drawer at the bottom, as the sister apps do. In the narrow panel the Building blocks table stacks each block's row: the id, the name and the level on top, the other columns under them, each with its column's name.

**The tiles.** Up to 1,700 px the tiles have a row of their own (§6). A tile's note wraps to two lines and is never cut: the weakest-link tile names its block, and where the block's name does not fit the note's two lines (a long name, a narrow tile, German), the note keeps the block's id and the tile's tooltip gives the name.

**The spider's labels and values.** The layer view fits its web to its labels: each label's edges are worked out from its lines, its status line included, each line measured in the page's own font, and the web takes the largest radius at which every label stays inside the drawing, moving its centre up or down where that buys room. On a map too short for the full labels (a small screen, German) the axes take the layers' short names, as PLAN.md f.2 has it, with the status line kept and the full name in the axis's tooltip, its accessible name and the table; where the web is still too small, the labels take the smaller size. The block view sets its labels in two columns, one each side of the web, in their axes' order: labels move apart until none overlaps the next and stand wholly outside the web, a label moved away from its axis end is joined to it by a thin line in `--vc-border-strong`, and a block's name wraps at 24 characters at most, a long compound after one of its hyphens, so one long name does not take the web's room. On a narrow map the block view's labels take the small size, and where even that leaves the web too small, only their first lines, the id and the two codes. When a column cannot hold the full labels, every label keeps its first line, the id and the two codes, and the web grows into the room the names no longer take. With more than twelve blocks the values beside the markers give way to the rings and their level ticks; the honeycomb and the table keep the numbers. A value stands beside its marker, across its axis; in the block view a value in the outer quarter stands inward along its axis, so it stays inside the web and away from the labels, and a value at the centre moves out along its own axis, so several zeros do not pile up there. Markers at the centre lie on one another, so their shared value, the "0" of several blocks at level 0, is written once, beside them along one of their axes. No value covers another value, a marker of any axis or a label; Compare's second series finds its places around the first. The ticks run up the first axis: the outer ring, the top of the scale, has none, and a tick gives way to any value that would cover it. On a short map the block view also takes the honeycomb's room, because every block has its own axis there.

**The honeycomb.** Its cells are buttons and stay 40 px targets at every size: 46 by 40 px where the map is short, with the level written and the id and codes left to the cell's name. A group's score or status moves under the layer's name when the group is narrow. Where a run has more blocks than a short map can show (thirty blocks at 1280×720), the map scrolls rather than cutting a row of cells off.

**The keyboard.** A whole run works by keyboard alone: Tab reaches the service field, the arrow keys and Enter pick an entry and start, Tab and Enter choose Simulation, confirm the review and approve; the arrow keys and Space choose a lever. Every control that takes the focus shows it: Vici's focus ring in `--vc-focus`, which is merlot (§3); on Vici's icon buttons and radio buttons the ring is the hexagon they are drawn as. The skip link slides in above the header.

**Screen readers.** The spider diagram is a group named by its title and description, and each axis in it is an image named with its label and its value ("Infrastructure (German: Infrastruktur): 100"); the rings, the area and the markers are hidden from screen readers beside those names. Each honeycomb cell is named with its block, its level and both codes in words. Every quote carries the language it was read in, in the evidence of the Building blocks tab and in the report's sources, so a German quote in an English report is read in German.

**What a missing score says.** A layer without a score says why, in the spider's status line, the honeycomb's head and the table: "not researched yet", "no evidence found", "not applicable", "no evidence on maker or operator" ("Herkunft nicht belegt") where its blocks have evidence but no maker or operator on record, so that none has a level, and "not assessed" on every layer of a run that ended without an assessment, whose map line says so too and whose progress rail marks scoring, the review and the recommendation as not needed. The overall tile says on how many of the six layers its score rests when that is fewer ("indicative; 3 of 6 layers scored"), and so do Compare's table and the Portfolio's bars. The weakest-link tile's tooltip says that it counts only the critical blocks, so a level-0 block that is important or supporting, on screen beside it, does not read as a contradiction. An assessment older than ninety days says "Check for updates" on the map and in Compare's and the Portfolio's names for it.

**The review form and the console in German.** The correction form shows an unknown party's name in the interface's language ("unbekannt") and an unknown country as an empty field whose placeholder says so, never as the recorded "unknown" or "UNKNOWN"; a line above the blocks spells out the origin codes ("UNB unbekannt" and the rest), which elsewhere sit in tooltips. A tool call's details in the console are the call's raw record, in the tools' English field names, under a line in the interface's language that says so and points to the Building blocks tab, where a block's evidence reads in the person's language; the record itself carries `lang="en"`.

**The report's blocks.** The report ends its own part with a table of the building blocks (id, layer, name, criticality, made in, run by, level), as the map's table has it, so the ids its findings and figures use are explained in the file itself; the memo names its weakest link by id and name.

**Motion.** With the reader's setting for reduced motion, the fjord outline of a search or a fetch stands still instead of pulsing; nothing else in the map moves.

**How it is held.** `tests/layout.mjs` measures five sizes from 1920×1080 to 1280×720 in both themes and both languages through seven states of a run, its block view among them, the first seconds, the review, the end and the block view of six pre-runs at 1280×720 and 1366×768, every assessed pre-run in German at 1536×864, 1366×768 and 1280×720, the table view, a run of thirty blocks, and the dialogs, with every label, value, marker and tick apart; `tests/design.mjs` checks the colour grammar above in the running app in both themes, reduced motion, and writes captures for a projector washout and for protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia; `tests/accept.mjs` ("keyboard") makes the run by keyboard and checks every focus; `tests/smoke.mjs` ("names") reads the names and the languages.
